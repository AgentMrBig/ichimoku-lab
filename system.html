The System

One trading engine — a high‑probability reversal scalper — running on one always‑on server, executing on a single MetaTrader 4 account through a custom Expert Advisor. It isn't a strategy borrowed from a book. It's one trader's own hand, made tireless.

The thesis

The trader behind this had a real edge by hand: small, high‑probability moves, taken with far more size than normal — precisely because the move was small and the probability was high. The flaw was never the entries; it was that a rare bad trade had no floor. This system is that exact style, systematized, with the one thing the hand never had: a hard, bounded stop on every trade. High win rate, tiny bounded losses, size pressed where the odds justify it.

The engine — Fade 1×9

what it does

Catch the exhaustion, take the snap‑back

It hunts moves that have overextended into a level and stalled — then fades them, betting on the quick reversion. It doesn't wait for a big trend; it takes a fast, small, high‑odds profit and gets out.

  • Magic 12345 · EUR/USD · USD/JPY · gold
  • Fires 9 units of 1% per signal (the "1×9")
  • Quick‑profit target: 0.5R · hard stop: −1R

how it sees

Two timeframes, then the trigger

The setup arms on the M15/M30 structure — support/resistance levels, a 9‑EMA spine, Bollinger rejection, a climax filter. The entry is then timed on the 1‑minute: 9/20 MA convergence → a 20‑MA break → a prior‑swing break. Arm slow, fire fast.

  • Trend‑gated: won't fade a short into an uptrend (above the 200‑MA)
  • Per‑pair cooldown against over‑stacking
  • 16‑bar time‑stop clears anything that stalls

Because it wins so often and loses so small, the size can be pressed — the 1×9 splits one signal across nine positions, both to press size and to slip under the broker's per‑position lot ceiling. That combination — high win rate, bounded loss, pressed size — is what bends the equity curve so steeply while keeping drawdown low.

How a trade actually happens

The strategy logic lives in a Node.js "bridge"; MetaTrader is just the hands that place the orders. They talk through files on disk.

MT4 + EA log the ticks. The Expert Advisor, attached to the 3 charts, writes every tick to ticks_<SYMBOL>.csv in MT4's Files folder.
The bridge reads the ticks and rebuilds the candles it needs — M15/M30 for the arm, M1 for the trigger.
It computes the signal — reversal confluence — and applies the gates: trend filter, per‑pair cooldown, spread guard (stands down when spreads blow out at the 5pm‑NY rollover).
It writes an order file (orders_<SYMBOL>.json) with side, stop, 0.5R target, risk %, and units — and pings Discord.
The EA executes it next tick: sizes the lots off the live balance, opens the 9 positions, sets each stop and take‑profit.
The EA manages the exit — take‑profit at +0.5R, hard stop at −1R, time‑stop after 16 bars — keyed by magic number, independent of the bridge.

Risk is a percent of the current balance, so wins compound. And the goal isn't a big number in the broker — it's throughput: grow fast enough to siphon the winnings out regularly, into the real world. The account is a faucet, not a vault.

The stack

Everything lives on one small always‑on Linux VPS so trades never miss when the home PC is off. MetaTrader (a Windows app) runs under Wine; the bridge runs natively.

Hostinger KVM · Ubuntu 24.04 Wine 9 · MT4 portable VisionBridgeEA · the EA Node.js · the fade bridge pm2 · process manager Xvfb + noVNC · headless display

Every piece is a boot‑persistent service — a reboot brings the whole stack back, MT4 auto‑logs‑in, and the engine resumes. Remote control is command‑line over SSH; the MT4 desktop is reachable in a browser through a tunneled noVNC session.

Two things we retired — honestly

benched

The Ichimoku trend engine

Kaiten started as two engines — this reversal scalper plus a slow Ichimoku trend follower for diversification. In review we found a trailing‑stop bug that had been inflating the trend engine's backtest (a stop placed above price, booking fills that never happened). Corrected, its real edge was marginal. So it's parked — magic 12347, process stopped — pending an honest rebuild or a better swing engine. We'd rather run one clean engine than two where one is a mirage.

dormant

The NFP grabber

A separate component (magic 12346) that arms an OCO straddle around high‑impact news releases and otherwise sleeps. Not part of the live money machine — it only wakes for scheduled events.

Reading the backtest honestly. The $43.7M figure is a discrete‑lot backtest (2023–2025) that compounds aggressively; the absolute dollars are a projection, not a promise. Two caveats we hold in front of us: it's an M15 model, while the live engine enters on M1 — real fills may shave the edge; and the 0.5R quick target is thin on gold, where it's roughly the size of the spread, so gold is the pair we watch first. The point of running it live on demo is to measure exactly how much of the edge survives. Trust the drawdown shape and the win rate; verify the dollars in forward test.
