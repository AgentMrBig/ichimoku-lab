The 10% Curve

The high‑win‑rate fade (98% win, bounded −1R loss) sized at 10% risk per trade, split across positions to bypass the 100‑lot cap. Early on the split compounds cleanly. Once a salvo would need more lots than the market can realistically fill, we deploy the max feasible instead — effective risk decays, and exponential growth hands off to a linear grind. This is the honest version: no fantasy trillions, a real ceiling.

How to read it. The teal curve is equity on a log scale; the dashed line marks where the split hit the fill ceiling and growth turned from exponential to linear. The red panel below is drawdown — it never breaches the start, because the loss is bounded (−1R) and rare (worst streak of 1 in two years). The catch is the ceiling assumption (~500 fillable lots per trade): raise it and the curve runs hotter, lower it and it flattens sooner. And the whole floor rests on losses staying isolated — a live 2–3 loss cluster at 10% is a −19% to −27% dip, survivable but real. Research only — not the live system.
