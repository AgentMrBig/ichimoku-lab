The H1 Finding

The Ichimoku engine sets its bias on the daily chart and times entries on a lower one. It has always timed on the H4. This study asks whether that's right — and finds the engine has been leaving most of its edge on the table. Dropping every pair to H1 roughly 2.3× the engine's total return. All figures are R-space on 2 years of 1-minute data (2023–2025), charged at the broker's real measured spreads.

The takeaway. The engine has been throttled on H4 the whole time. At the broker's real spreads, H1 lifts every pair — EUR/USD 4×, USD/JPY 2.5×, gold 1.3× — and the edge gets stronger in the out-of-sample half, so it isn't a curve fit. The only cost is on gold, where H1 trades more heat (drawdown 9→17R) for its extra return; the majors gain with no such tradeoff. Earlier caution about gold on H1 came from stress-testing at 2–3× spread, which the broker's actual tick data shows never happens outside the rollover window. Not yet live — the case is made; a demo run confirms live fills match before the switch.
